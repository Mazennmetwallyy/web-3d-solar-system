Solar System Explorer

What I made, why I made it that way, and what went wrong.

What I built

Project Overview

It's a solar system app with two main pages. The home page has all eight planets orbiting the Sun in 3D and you can click on any of them to go to the Explore page, where you can spin the planet around, change how the surface looks, and read information about it that gets loaded from a PHP backend. I went with a solar system theme mainly because planets are just spheres so I didn't have to deal with complicated geometry and could focus more on the shaders and materials instead.

I used THREE.js for the 3D rendering, PHP with SQLite for the backend, Bootstrap for layout, and the Web Audio API for background sound. All the planet textures are drawn with Canvas 2D when the page loads so there are no image files in the project.

3D Models

Blender – Solar System X3D Models

I used Blender 4 with its Python API (bpy) to build the 3D models. I wrote a script that creates the whole scene programmatically — it adds each planet as a UV sphere, applies materials and textures through the node editor, then exports everything as X3D files so they can be opened in a browser or X3D viewer.

For the surfaces I used Noise Texture nodes on the rocky planets (Mercury, Earth, Mars) and Wave Texture nodes for Jupiter and Saturn to get the banding effect. Saturn and Uranus have ring geometry created with a Boolean Difference so the inner hole is properly cut out rather than just a flat circle. The Sun has an Emission Strength of 5 and a Point Light at its centre to actually illuminate the scene.

Design decisions

Why I made certain choices

Dark background was kind of a given since the 3D scene takes up the whole screen — a light background would just look wrong. I went with a gold accent colour because it felt space-themed and contrasts well enough to be readable. Bootstrap handles the grid and the nav, everything else is custom CSS using variables so I could change colours easily without hunting through the whole stylesheet.

The side panels have a blur effect so you can still see the 3D behind them. On mobile they slide off-screen and come back with the toggle button. There's a flicker bug in Safari iOS when you're rotating the planet and the panel is open — I half fixed it but couldn't fully get rid of it, it seems to be a known browser issue.

For the backend I tried to keep it structured — Planet.php does all the database stuff, api.php handles routing, and the JS just fetches and updates the scene. There's also a static fallback so it works even if there's no PHP server.

Accessibility

What I considered

Making a WebGL canvas properly accessible is difficult because it's basically just a picture at the end of the day. I focused on what I could actually do. All three pages have a skip link that appears when you tab to it. I used proper semantic elements like <main> and <aside> rather than stacking divs, and the info panel has aria-live so screen readers get told when it updates. The toggle buttons have aria-pressed so their state is communicated properly.

The Explore page works with just the keyboard — left and right arrows cycle through planets. I also added prefers-reduced-motion support so orbits pause automatically if someone has that enabled. I checked all the text colours against WCAG AA and had to bump up the muted text because it was failing at small sizes.

What I struggled with

The parts that took longer than expected

These are the specific things that didn't work first time and what I had to do to fix them.

GLSL shaders

I hadn't written GLSL before so this took ages. I wrote three shaders — the Sun surface, the atmosphere glow, and the background nebula.

The atmosphere one was the worst. The glow was showing up on the wrong side of the planet and I couldn't figure out why for a long time. Turned out I was comparing the surface normal against the camera direction but the normal was in eye space (from THREE.js's normalMatrix) and the camera direction was in world space, so the comparison made no sense. Found the explanation on an old Stack Overflow post eventually. Once I changed the coordinate space it just worked.

Alpha not working how I expected

When I was drawing the Mars texture I put a load of soft overlapping circles at 0.5 opacity to make it look rough and dusty. They all came out as a solid black blob. The problem is that transparent layers compound — they don't just add — so 37 circles at 0.5 opacity ends up basically fully opaque. I had to work out the actual per-circle alpha I needed to get the total darkness I wanted, which came out at around 0.012 each. It's not something that gets mentioned anywhere, you just hit it and wonder why nothing's working.

Bloom messing up the background

I added bloom so the Sun would glow properly. What I didn't realise is that the bloom threshold, the tone mapping exposure, and the nebula shader are all connected. I turned up the exposure to make the planets look better and suddenly the whole background turned purple because bloom was picking up the nebula output. Had to tune all three at the same time, you can't just change one and expect the others to stay normal.

Moons jumping when planets moved

My first approach was to calculate the moon's world position every frame with trig. It worked fine until the planet moved, then the moon would just teleport to the wrong spot.

The fix is to put an empty Object3D inside the planet's orbit pivot and attach the moon to that. When you rotate the empty object the moon follows automatically, no manual position maths needed. Obvious once you know, but I wasted a fair bit of time on the wrong approach before I got there.

Loading screen hanging with no server

When the PHP server wasn't running, the fetch just hung forever and the loading screen never went away. There was no error, the page just looked frozen. I fixed it with AbortController to cancel the request after 2.5 seconds and fall back to hardcoded JSON. Same thing happened with CDN textures so I ended up just generating everything procedurally with Canvas — which also means the app has no external dependencies to break.

Testing

What I tested and how

I tested in Chrome and Firefox on my laptop and on an iPhone in Safari. I also tested keyboard navigation without touching the mouse at all. The Safari blur flicker came up during the mobile testing and is only partially fixed.
